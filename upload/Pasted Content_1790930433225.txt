The key upgrade is to change the product from a generic healthcare SaaS into a **large-scale clinical knowledge, retrieval, medication-safety, and multilingual patient-guidance platform**. I’ll also make the architecture explicitly capable of handling the “many doctors repeatedly treating the same common problems” use case, while preventing the system from turning frequency of prescribing into automatic medical truth.

# MASTER BUILD PROMPT

## AI Clinical Knowledge + Medicine Guidance + Multilingual Voice Healthcare Platform

### India-First | Three Clinical Wings | ~5,000 Common Issues | Clinician-Governed | RAG + LangGraph + LangChain + LangFlow + Gemini + Voice + Playwright MCP

---

# 0. PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Build a production-grade healthcare application for India that helps patients understand common health issues, discover clinician-reviewed treatment information, understand medicines and their usage, and communicate through multilingual voice.

The platform is designed around a core observation:

> A very large number of doctors repeatedly encounter the same common patient complaints and prescribe or recommend treatments according to their clinical judgment.

For example, many general physicians may independently manage recurring complaints such as:

* stomach pain
* gas / bloating
* acidity
* indigestion
* constipation
* headache
* fever
* cough
* cold
* body pain
* vomiting
* diarrhea
* weakness
* allergy-related symptoms
* skin complaints
* sleep-related complaints
* and thousands of other common health issues.

The product should identify and organize approximately **5,000 common health issues**, then connect each issue to a structured, clinician-governed knowledge model containing:

* symptoms
* possible causes
* warning signs
* questions to ask
* investigations that may be relevant
* medicine information
* medicine classes
* active ingredients
* indications
* contraindications
* interactions
* age considerations
* pregnancy considerations where applicable
* common usage information
* timing information
* duration information where clinically approved
* food-related instructions where applicable
* side effects
* precautions
* follow-up requirements
* escalation conditions
* source provenance
* clinician review
* evidence level
* modality/care-wing context

The application must **not assume that the medicine prescribed most frequently is automatically the correct medicine**.

Frequency is a signal for knowledge discovery and analytics.

Clinical authority must come from:

1. approved clinical knowledge;
2. qualified clinician review;
3. applicable guidelines/reference sources;
4. deterministic safety rules;
5. jurisdictional/regulatory requirements;
6. patient-specific contraindications and risk factors.

```text
WING 1 — ALLOPATHY / MODERN MEDICINE
```

* practitioners

* medicine catalog

* clinical workflows
* contraindications
* source documents
* review processes
* AI policies

* prompts

* regulatory requirements

The same patient complaint may appear across all three wings, but the system must preserve the distinction.

Example:

```text
PATIENT ISSUE
"Stomach discomfort"

             ↓

       ISSUE NORMALIZATION

       ↙        ↓        ↘

ALLOPATHY   AYURVEDA   HOMEOPATHY
    ↓           ↓           ↓
Knowledge    Knowledge   Knowledge
    ↓           ↓           ↓
Medicines    Remedies    Medicines
```

Never combine results into a single recommendation without explicitly preserving the clinical wing.

# 3. IMPORTANT CLINICAL GOVERNANCE PRINCIPLE

The platform must NOT implement:

> "AI finds the medicine that 1.2 lakh doctors prescribe and tells the patient to take it."

Instead implement:

> "The platform identifies common clinical patterns and presents clinician-reviewed, evidence-linked medicine and care information appropriate to the selected clinical wing and patient context."

The system must distinguish:

```text
POPULARITY
FREQUENCY
CLINICIAN PRACTICE PATTERN
EVIDENCE
SAFETY
PATIENT-SPECIFIC SUITABILITY
```

These are separate concepts.

A medicine with high prescription frequency must never automatically outrank a clinically safer or more appropriate alternative.
